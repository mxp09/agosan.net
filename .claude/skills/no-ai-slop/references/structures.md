# Structures to Avoid

## Binary Contrasts

The "Not X. But Y." formula is one of the most recognizable AI patterns. It creates a false sense of revelation by setting up something to knock down.

| Pattern | Why it's a problem |
|---------|--------------------|
| "Not because X. Because Y." | Telegraphed reversal |
| "[X] isn't the problem. [Y] is." | Formulaic reframe |
| "The answer isn't X. It's Y." | Predictable pivot |
| "It feels like X. It's actually Y." | Setup/reveal cliche |
| "The question isn't X. It's Y." | Rhetorical misdirection |
| "stops being X and starts being Y" | False transformation arc |
| "doesn't mean X, but actually Y" | Negation-then-assertion |
| "not just X but also Y" | Additive hedge |

**Fix:** Either state Y directly ("The problem is Y.") or join the contrast into a single sentence using "but rather", "but", or "instead": "The problem isn't the tooling but rather the team's communication." Splitting a contrast across two sentences with periods is the AI tell — a conjunction in one sentence is how people actually write.

## Negative Listing

Listing what something is *not* before revealing what it *is*. This is the binary contrast stretched into a runway.

- "Not a X... Not a Y... A Z."
- "It wasn't X. It wasn't Y. It was Z."

**Fix:** State Z. Skip the buildup.

## Dramatic Fragmentation

Sentence fragments deployed for manufactured profundity:

- "[Noun]. That's it. That's the [thing]."
- "X. And Y. And Z." (staccato drama)
- "This unlocks something. [Single word]."
- "Collaboration deepens. Innovation accelerates. Culture shifts." (a sequence of short sentences that belong together)

**Fix:** First try joining into one sentence with conjunctions. Then score the result for fluency (1-5). If it reads naturally (4-5), keep it: "Collaboration deepens and innovation accelerates as the culture changes" works because the ideas share a subject and direction. If it reads like clauses glued together (1-3), rephrase from scratch instead, preserving the original meaning but rewording freely. Signs of a low-scoring join: multiple comma-separated clauses with different subjects, no clear logical link between parts, or weak ", and" bridging unrelated ideas. The staccato version reads like a slide deck, but a bad join reads like a slide deck with the bullets crammed onto one line.

## Rhetorical Setups

These announce that an insight is coming instead of delivering it:

- "What if [reframe]?"
- "Here's what I mean:"
- "Think about it:"
- "And that's okay."
- "Imagine this:"
- "Consider this:"

**Fix:** Make the point. If the insight is good, it stands alone.

## False Agency

Giving inanimate things human verbs. AI does this to avoid naming who actually did something.

| Pattern | What's actually happening |
|---------|--------------------------|
| "a complaint becomes a fix" | Someone fixed it |
| "a bet lives or dies in days" | Someone killed the project or shipped it |
| "the decision emerges" | Someone decided |
| "the culture shifts" | People changed behavior |
| "the conversation moves toward" | Someone steered it |
| "the data tells us" | Someone read it and drew a conclusion |
| "the market rewards" | Buyers paid for something |
| "the technology enables" | Engineers built something that lets people... |

**Fix:** Name the person. "The team fixed it that week" beats "the complaint becomes a fix." When no specific person fits, use "you."

## Narrator-from-a-Distance

Observing the scene from above instead of putting the reader inside it:

- "Nobody designed this."
- "This happens because..."
- "This is why..."
- "People tend to..."
- "There's a reason..."
- "It's no coincidence that..."

**Fix:** Put the reader in the room. "You don't sit down one day and decide to..." beats "Nobody designed this."

## Passive Voice

Every sentence needs someone doing something. Passive voice hides the actor.

| Passive | Active |
|---------|--------|
| "X was created" | "[Person] created X" |
| "It is believed that" | "[Person] believes" |
| "Mistakes were made" | "[Person] made mistakes" |
| "The decision was reached" | "[Person] decided" |
| "Progress has been made" | "[Team] made progress" |

**Fix:** Find the actor. Put them first.

## Sentence Starters

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| What, When, Where, Which, Who, Why, How | Wh- openers become a crutch. "What makes this hard is..." should be "The constraint is..." or better: name the constraint. |
| "So" at paragraph start | Conversational filler. Start with content. |
| "Look," | Performative directness. Just be direct. |
| "Now," | Unnecessary transition. |

## Rhythm Patterns

| Pattern | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Three-item lists | Use two items, or one |
| Questions answered immediately | Let the question breathe, or cut it |
| Every paragraph ends with a punchy line | Vary your endings |
| Em dashes | Remove. Use commas or periods. |
| Inline colons | "Three traits: discipline, timing, and feedback." or "It comes down to one thing: trust." Colons should only appear before bullet-point or numbered lists. For inline lists, use "like", "such as", "including", or fold the items into the sentence naturally. |
| Staccato fragments stacked together | Don't pile short sentences for effect |
| "Not always. Not perfectly." | Hedging disguised as reassurance. Cut it. |

## Word-Level Patterns

| Pattern | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Lazy extremes: every, always, never, everyone, nobody | Use specifics. Who, exactly? When, exactly? |
| Adverb pileup | See `phrases.md`. Cut them. |
| "Transform" / "transformative" | Usually an exaggeration. Say what actually changed. |
| "Powerful" (as adjective for ideas/tools) | Overused to the point of meaninglessness. Be specific about *how* it's effective. |
