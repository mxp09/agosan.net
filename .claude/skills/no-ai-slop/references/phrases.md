# Phrases to Remove

## Throat-Clearing Openers

These announce the point instead of making it. Cut them and start with the content.

- "Here's the thing:"
- "Here's what [X]" / "Here's this [X]" / "Here's that [X]" / "Here's why [X]"
- "The uncomfortable truth is"
- "It turns out"
- "The real [X] is"
- "Let me be clear"
- "The truth is,"
- "I'll say it again:"
- "I'm going to be honest"
- "Can we talk about"
- "Here's what I find interesting"
- "Here's the problem though"

Any "here's what/this/that" construction is a delay before the point. Cut to the point.

## Emphasis Crutches

These try to make a statement feel important without adding information. Delete them.

- "Full stop." / "Period."
- "Let that sink in."
- "This matters because"
- "Make no mistake"
- "Here's why that matters"
- "Read that again."
- "I can't stress this enough"

## Business Jargon

Replace with plain language:

| Instead of | Write |
|------------|-------|
| Navigate (challenges) | Handle, deal with |
| Unpack (analysis) | Explain, examine |
| Lean into | Accept, try |
| Landscape (context) | Situation, field |
| Game-changer | Important, significant |
| Double down | Commit, increase |
| Deep dive | Analysis, close look |
| Take a step back | Reconsider |
| Moving forward | Next, from now on |
| Circle back | Return to, revisit |
| On the same page | Aligned, agreed |
| Level up | Improve |
| Synergy | Cooperation |
| Low-hanging fruit | Easy wins |
| Move the needle | Make a difference |

## Adverbs

Cut all of these. If the sentence doesn't survive without the adverb, the sentence needs a rewrite, not a crutch.

Specific repeat offenders: really, just, literally, genuinely, honestly, simply, actually, deeply, truly, fundamentally, inherently, inevitably, interestingly, importantly, crucially, incredibly, extremely, absolutely, undeniably, remarkably, profoundly.

## Filler Phrases

These burn words without adding meaning:

- "At its core"
- "In today's [X]"
- "It's worth noting"
- "At the end of the day"
- "When it comes to"
- "In a world where"
- "The reality is"
- "It goes without saying"
- "Needless to say"
- "For what it's worth"
- "To be fair"
- "That said" / "That being said"
- "With that in mind"
- "All things considered"

## Listicle Transitions

These turn prose into a numbered list wearing a disguise. They signal "I'm about to make my next point" instead of just making it:

- "Finally,"
- "First," / "Second," / "Third,"
- "Additionally,"
- "Furthermore,"
- "Moreover,"
- "On top of that,"
- "Last but not least,"
- "Another key factor is"

If you need transitions, let the content create them naturally. A new paragraph already signals a new idea.

## Meta-Commentary

The text should move forward, not narrate its own structure:

- "Hint:" / "Plot twist:" / "Spoiler:"
- "You already know this, but"
- "But that's another post"
- "X is a feature, not a bug"
- "Dressed up as"
- "The rest of this essay explains..."
- "Let me walk you through..."
- "In this section, we'll..."
- "As we'll see..."
- "I want to explore..."
- "Let's break this down"
- "Let's dive in"

## Performative Sincerity

Phrases that manufacture intimacy or emphasize by insisting:

- "creeps in"
- "I promise"
- "They exist, I promise"
- "This is genuinely hard"
- "This is what leadership actually looks like"
- "actually matters"
- "If I'm being honest"

## Vague Declaratives

Sentences that announce importance without naming the specific thing. Replace with the specific thing, or cut entirely:

- "The reasons are structural"
- "The implications are significant"
- "This is the deepest problem"
- "The stakes are high"
- "The consequences are real"
- "This changes everything"
- "That's a big deal"
